#+title: org-foresight
#+options: toc:nil

Forward-looking capacity, signals and scheduling for Org.

Org records the past well and the future badly. Clock reports, logbooks and
agenda logs all answer /what happened/; almost nothing answers /what can still
be promised/. =org-foresight= adds that missing axis.

* What it answers

- *How much can I still take on today?* — available time, minus what is already
  committed, minus a reserve for work that has not arrived yet.
- *When will I be done?* — the remaining commitment poured into the gaps between
  meetings, which gives a finish time rather than a hope.
- *When could I take this on?* — the same calculation over the next fortnight,
  which is what turns "I'm busy" into a date.

* First use

** See it working before configuring anything

#+begin_src
M-x org-foresight-demo-mode
#+end_src

Generates a week of tasks, meetings, travel, clock history and one example of
every signal; points the agenda at it; and puts everything back when switched
off. Real files are never touched. Then =C-c a a= for the day, =C-c a p= for
the plan board.

=M-x org-foresight-demo-regenerate= returns the demo to a clean slate after
experimenting.

** Where the settings go

In an =init.el= or a module, not =custom.el=. Every value below says something
about how you work, and the reasoning is worth keeping next to it — Customize
records the value and loses the reason.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(use-package org-foresight
  :after org-agenda
  :config
  ;; The shape of an ordinary day.  A declaration, not an observation: the
  ;; end time is the hour you intend to leave, so that there is something to
  ;; defend.  M-x org-foresight-shape-day declares an unusual one.
  (setq org-foresight-awake         '("06:30" . "23:00")
        org-foresight-workday-start "09:00"
        org-foresight-workday-end   "17:30"
        org-foresight-workdays      '(1 2 3 4 5))

  ;; Where entries happen, and what moving between them costs.  Without this
  ;; no journey is ever counted and every office meeting looks an hour long.
  (setq org-foresight-places '((office . "HQ\\|Meeting Room")
                               (client . "Client\\|Site"))
        org-foresight-home-place 'home
        org-foresight-travel-matrix '(((home . office) . 60)
                                      ((office . client) . 45))
        org-foresight-travel-default 45)

  ;; Categories that are life rather than work.  They fill the day but never
  ;; count against capacity, and are not reported as work in the evening.
  (setq org-foresight-private-categories '("family" "personal"))

  ;; Meetings from this category imply preparation and follow-up.
  (setq org-foresight-meeting-categories '("work"))

  ;; Where generated tasks and per-day declarations are written.
  (setq org-foresight-task-file (expand-file-name "journal.org" org-directory)
        org-foresight-day-file  (expand-file-name "journal.org" org-directory))

  (add-hook 'org-agenda-finalize-hook #'org-foresight-report-render))
#+end_src

=M-x org-foresight-diagnose= then reports what is set, what is having no
effect, and what to do next. Worth running once after the above and again
whenever a figure looks wrong.

Note the =:LOCATION:= lines. Journeys are derived from where the day happens,

What to look for: the office meeting costs its journey either side, the remote

as unplannable because it has no estimate.

** The daily loop

*During the day* — =C-c a a=. Org draws the day; org-foresight hands it what
no file contains:

 Work 8:30 · OVER by 3:45 · will not fit · est ×1.1

            █ booked 2:45  █ travel 2:00  █ promised 7:30  ▭ surge 1:00

 Off   7:00 ███████··························
            █ private 1:30  · unclaimed 5:30

Thursday   13 August 2026
          08:00...... ----------------
          09:00...... work starts ────────────
          09:00-09:30 0:30 free · 0:26 usable
  outlook 09:30-09:45 Morning standup
          09:45-12:00 2:15 free · 1:59 usable
  procure 09:45...... ↳ NEXT Write the vendor comparison 2:00
          10:00...... ----------------
          12:00...... ----------------
  travel  12:00-13:00 → office
  outlook 13:00-14:00 All-hands
          14:00...... ----------------
  outlook 14:00-15:30 Project review
  travel  15:00-15:45 ⨯ → client
          15:29...... ← now ──────────────────────────
  outlook 15:45-16:30 Client review
  travel  16:15-17:30 ⨯ → home
          17:30...... work ends ──────────────
  family  19:00-20:30 Dinner with the family
✗ reporti Scheduled:  6:00 NEXT Rewrite the annual report

Clocked · Estimate · Observed …
#+end_example

The rules, the entries, =now=, the categories, the effort column — all of
that is Org. Four things are not:

| Row                | What it is                                        |
|--------------------+---------------------------------------------------|
| =→ office=         | a journey derived from where the day happens      |
| =0:30 free=        | a stretch nothing has claimed, and what survives the reserve |
| =↳ NEXT …=         | promised work that would fit in the gap above it  |
| =work starts ────= | the edge of the working day                       |

Three marks, explained by a key under the bars — and only the ones the day
actually used, because a key describing a clash on a day that has none is
explaining a problem the reader does not have:

| Mark | On                    | Meaning                                |
|------+-----------------------+----------------------------------------|
| =✗=  | undated work          | no gap today is big enough to hold it  |
| =⨯=  | a journey             | it cannot have the time it needs       |
| =↳=  | a candidate           | it would fit in the gap above          |

None of them is written to a file, and none of them is placed for you.

*** Why Org draws it

The agenda already is the day: the time grid interleaves its rules with every
timed entry, in order, with =now= in its place. Drawing a second column beside
it would mean drawing the day twice — and the copy would lose the log lines,
the habit graphs, the tags, the dimming of blocked tasks, and every command
that already works on a real agenda line.

So the derived rows are handed to Org rather than drawn: each is built by
`org-agenda-format-item' with a time and no entry behind it — exactly how Org
builds its own grid rules — and added before `org-agenda-finalize-entries'
sorts. Org puts each one where its clock says it goes. Nothing here reproduces
Org's ordering, and nothing breaks when a sorting option changes.

*Every added row must carry a time.* That is the whole contract, and it is
worth stating because the failure is quiet: `org-cmp-time' reads the
=time-of-day= property and treats a row without one as later than everything,
and since the sort is stable such rows come out in the order they were added
— in a heap at the foot of the buffer, outside the day they describe. A test
holds every row to it.

A journey that has been squeezed is drawn at the length it *needs* rather than
at what was left for it, marked =⨯=. Fifteen minutes shown for a forty-five
minute drive is the day claiming to work when it does not; drawn honestly it
overlaps the meeting it collides with, which is where the collision is.

A candidate row carries the marker of the entry it names, so =s=, =S-right=,
=>=, =e=, =t= and =RET= work on it. That is the point of showing candidates at
the hour a gap opens: the answer to "where does this go" is given by putting
the cursor on the line and pressing the key you would have pressed anyway.

Set =org-foresight-agenda-inject= to nil to see the agenda exactly as Org
builds it.

*** The gap, net of the reserve

A gap is offered at what survives interruption rather than at what the clock
says: =free 3:15 · 2:53 after the reserve=.

Interruptions do not happen at a fixed hour, so putting the reserve down as a
block somewhere would be a claim about the day that is not true. What *is*
true is that they scale with time worked — so every gap is offered at the
fraction of itself that history says survives, and the reserve stops being an
abstraction subtracted somewhere out of sight.

Nothing is placed either way. The gap is only being reported honestly, and
what goes in it stays entirely yours to decide. =org-foresight-gap-net= nil
offers you the clock instead.

*** What will not fit at all

=✗= marks work with no gap today big enough to hold it.

That is a fact about the work and the day rather than about any arrangement of
them: if an estimate is longer than the largest gap there is, no ordering will
find it a home. Being decidable without placing anything is what keeps the
choice of where things go with you. The day-level version of the same question
— everything fits individually but not together — is the verdict's =OVER by=,
and the line under it.

*** The left edge

One rule, three levels, for the blocks org-foresight adds above and below the
listing:

| Column | What sits there                                              |
|--------+--------------------------------------------------------------|
|      0 | badges, and nothing else                                     |
|      1 | everything a section contains: verdicts, keys, bars, rows     |
|     2+ | subordinate to the line above — a signal's findings under its heading |

A badge is read by *scanning* rather than by reading, so it gets the frame
edge to itself: an eye running down the left of the buffer hits section
headings and nothing else. Everything a section contains starts one column
in, which is also where Org's agenda puts its lines. Depth past that means
one thing only: this line belongs to the one above it.

That is why the bars are not flush left. A bar is not a label; it is the
picture of the numbers beside it, and it belongs at the same margin as the
words it illustrates.

** What has to come out

The mirror of the same idea sits under the verdict. A bar says how far over
the day is; it can never say by what. So an overcommitted day names its way
out:

: Work 8:30 · OVER by 0:45 · will not fit
: ↳ any one of Quarterly summary 1:00 · Project review 1:30

Ranked by the *smallest that is on its own enough* — the opposite order to
the gap's, and for the same reason: there you want the most in, here you want
to give up the least. Where nothing alone is enough, the largest are taken in
turn until they are (=↳ needs all of=), because the question has stopped
being which one and become how many. Where even everything movable falls
short, it says so outright:

: ↳ only 3:00 of it can move: Draft the summary 1:30 · Standup 0:30

which is a different problem with a different answer. A day whose overflow is
made of other people's meetings cannot be rescheduled out of trouble, and
knowing that at 09:00 is the difference between declining something and
working late.

Meetings and dated tasks are candidates alongside promised work — a day is
rarely saved by moving only the things nobody else knows about. Travel is
not, because it is not moved: it follows whatever it was getting you to.
Neither is anything private or =informational=. An overfilled *working* day
is not answered by moving the family, and a fixture nobody asked you to
attend was never yours to move. =org-foresight-grid-frees= sets how many are
named, or =nil= for none.

Deadlines are Org's own — =org-agenda-deadline-leaders= writes =In 3 d.:= in
front of the entry, which asks the same question in the same column as
everything else.

** A note on the block characters

Three shapes, and each says something the colour cannot.

| Shape        | What it is                 | Segments                          |
|--------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------------|
| filled block | claimed                    | booked, travel, promised, private |
| outline      | held open, holding nothing | surge                             |
| dots         | not claimed at all         | spare, unclaimed, free            |

Only the claimed hours need telling apart from each other, so only they spend
the grey ramp — three steps, two apart, which is as fine as an eye reliably
resolves. Everything else is a shape, and shapes are read faster than shades.
(The examples render the outline as =▭= for want of a way to draw a face box
in a text file; on screen it is one rectangle around the whole run, in the
same yellow as =┃= and =OVER by=. The reserve is the last thing between a day
and an overrun, so spending it *is* being over without having said so.)

Nothing is lost by drawing the unclaimed part in dots, because a horizontal
bar carries its quantity in *length*, not in ink — the run is the same length
either way. What is gained is that the one thing on the board worth growing
stops shouting: a saturated hue at full block density outweighs the same area
of grey by far more than its share of the day, so a bar with plenty of room
in it reads as the busiest of the lot.

Shading the segments with =▓ ▒ ░= would be the obvious choice and is the
wrong one: in several monospace fonts those sit on a different box from =█=
— in PlemolJP, =▓= is 74 units high and 55 left of it on a 1000 em, and
narrower besides — so every seam between them visibly steps.

The part-width blocks =▏▎▍▌▋▊▉= are kept for what they are actually for: the
last cell of a run, where the duration does not fill a whole one. They share
the full block's box exactly, and nothing follows them to be pushed out of
line.

One thing to check in your own font: =█= is normally drawn to *overflow* its
em box, because the glyph exists to tile a screen without seams. Where it
does, a bar stands visibly taller than boxed text on the same line, and two
bars set one under the other fuse into one mass. Measure it — in an unpatched
PlemolJP the block runs 1300 units against a 1175-unit line, i.e. ~10% proud
at the bottom. Fonts that normalise tile glyphs to the line box (as a patched
PlemolJP NF does, at 890/−210) draw the bar flush with everything else.
That is a property of the font, not of this package.

* Not everything wants all of you

Occupying an hour and demanding all of it are different things, and treating
them as one makes a full day look impossible.

| =:ATTENTION:=   | Costs the hour | Shares it | Example                    |
|-----------------+----------------+-----------+----------------------------|
| (unset)         | yes            | no        | a meeting you run          |
| =background=    | yes            | yes       | a call you only listen to  |
| =informational= | no             | yes       | a child's fixture          |

A =background= entry keeps its hour but hangs beneath whatever else is
happening then, so listening on the commute stops being reported as a day
that cannot happen. An =informational= one takes nothing at all: it stays in
the agenda where it belongs, but claims none of the day and displaces no work
— a child's practice is not work, though it is exactly what decides when the
house is empty.

Set it per entry, or by category for things that are always so:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq org-foresight-informational-categories '("club"))
#+end_src

Then work down the board. Every row answers to the agenda's own commands, and
each edit moves the figures above it straight away:

| Key                     | What it does                     |
|-------------------------+----------------------------------|
| =s=                     | schedule it on another day       |
| =S-left= / =S-right=    | a day earlier or later           |
| =C-u S-left/right=      | an hour earlier or later         |
| =C-u C-u S-left/right=  | by =org-time-stamp-rounding-minutes= |
| =>=                     | prompt for a date, or a time     |
| =e=                     | estimate it                      |
| =t=                     | change its state                 |
| =RET=                   | visit the entry                  |

The time keys are what clear a =⨯=. They work because a booked row puts
=org-marker= on its timestamp rather than its heading, which is the agenda's
own convention — the commands go there and insist on finding a stamp. Set
=org-time-stamp-rounding-minutes= to =(0 15)= if five-minute steps are finer
than you think in.

- *Over?* Move something off today — the verdict names what would be enough,
  and the load table says which day can take it. Or move it *within* today,
  which is usually cheaper.
- *Spare?* Pull something in from the signals.

*During the day* — the first line of =C-c a a=. If =spare= is small, the
answer to a new request is a date, not a yes.

*Before leaving* — whatever review command you already use.

* The day

A day is not one bar from nine to half five. Work and private life are mixed in
time, some of it is spent getting somewhere, and the hours that belong to
neither are not absent — they are private time that happens to be unclaimed.

#+begin_example
 Work 8:30 · 1:15 left to promise · ends 16:45 · surge from 12 days · est ×1.4
            █ booked 2:45  █ travel 1:00  █ promised 2:30  ▭ surge 1:00
            · spare 1:15
 Work  8:30 ██████████████████████████████▭▭▭▭▭······
 Off   7:00 ███████··························
            █ private 1:30  · unclaimed 5:30
#+end_example

Two bars, written as the sums they are, so the parts can be checked against
the whole without adding them up and hoping. They share one scale: an hour is
the same length in both, which is what lets a working day be set against the
evening it leaves behind.

| Segment    | What it is                                          |
|------------+-----------------------------------------------------|
| =booked=   | meetings and work already at a time                 |
| =travel=   | getting to and from them                            |
| =private=  | life that falls inside working hours                |
| =promised= | effort accepted without a time yet                  |
| =surge=    | the reserve, for work that has not arrived          |
| =spare=    | the span less all five — what may still be promised |

The =Off= bar accounts for the rest of the waking day: =private= for
commitments outside working hours, =unclaimed= for the hours nothing has
claimed. Work that lands out there is not absorbed silently — it appears as
=borrowed=, and says so.

An overcommitted day is drawn past the width with =┃= where the span actually
ended, rather than being scaled back until it fits. The one day that must not
look workable is the one that is not.

"I leave at 17:30" is therefore not the end of a window. It is that the evening
is already spoken for.

The span is *declared*, never inferred. Measuring when work actually stopped
and calling that the working day would teach the system to plan around the late
nights it exists to prevent. =M-x org-foresight-shape-day= declares an unusual
day on its own heading; ordinary days need no input at all.

* Travel is work

Whether an employer counts the commute as paid time is beside the point: an
hour spent getting to a meeting is an hour of your life spent on that meeting.
Journeys are derived from where entries actually are and booked like anything
else.

#+begin_example
the same one-hour meeting, held remotely:   free 7:30 · spare 6:30
                          held at the office: free 5:30 · spare 4:30
#+end_example

Only a place someone wrote down counts. Every imported invitation carries a
video link, and a link says nothing about where the body has to be — so an
unhelpful =LOCATION= leaves you where you already were rather than inventing a
journey. Set =org-foresight-places= to map locations onto places, and
=org-foresight-travel-matrix= to say what moving between them costs.

* The reserve, and the estimates

Two corrections are learned from history, because neither can be got by asking
harder.

*Interruptions* are not controllable, but their volume is stable and
measurable. Time spent active at the machine with no clock running is work that
was done but never planned. =M-x org-foresight-learn-surge= takes the median of
that and subtracts it up front, so an interrupted day still ends on time. This
needs [[https://activitywatch.net/][ActivityWatch]]; without it the reserve falls back to a configured constant.

*Estimates* are wrong in a consistent direction per kind of work, and the
evidence is already in the file: every finished task that carried an =EFFORT=
and was clocked is one estimate measured against its outcome.
=M-x org-foresight-learn-bias= reads them back into a per-category multiplier
and applies it to what is promised and to how long a task is given when placed.

The multiplier is always shown (=est ×1.4=), so a shrinking day reads as "my
estimates are optimistic" rather than as the tool being pessimistic.

* Signals

Work that exists but has not been planned for. Shown on the plan board.

| Signal              | What it catches                                          |
|---------------------+----------------------------------------------------------|
| Impossible          | a journey that overlaps a meeting — the day as written    |
|                     | cannot happen, whatever effort is put into it             |
| After hours         | work parked outside the working window, so it is          |
|                     | subtracted from nothing and warns about nothing           |
| Won't fit today     | promised for today, and longer than any gap that remains  |
| Meetings, unprepared| an invitation implies preparation and follow-up that no    |
|                     | one has budgeted for                                      |
| Unplannable         | a near deadline with no estimate cannot be placed at all  |
| Gone quiet          | handed-off work whose check-in date has passed            |
| Kept moving         | repeated reschedules — a decision that is not being made  |
| Too much in flight  | several things started at once, each costing the switch    |
|                     | back into the others                                      |
| Borrowed            | too much of the week's work taken out of private time     |
| Leaking             | the reserve itself has grown large enough to be a problem |
| Orphaned prep       | preparation for a meeting that has been cancelled         |
| Undecided           | captured and never decided about (off by default: against |
|                     | a real journal it matched 17% of headings, mostly diary)  |

=M-x org-foresight-prepare-meetings= turns the second of these into real
tasks, filed either side of the meeting. It marks each meeting it handles, so
re-running creates nothing, and each generated task points back at its
meeting, which is what makes the orphan signal possible later.

* When, then

The forward view answers the second half of "I'm busy": /then when?/ It draws
the coming working days exactly as today is drawn above — same segments, same
colours, same scale — so the two can be read against each other:

#+begin_example
Load  when I could take this on
 Wed 08-12    OVER by 9:49  ████████████████████████████████████████┃███████…
 Thu 08-13  3:30 to promise  ███████████████████████████████████████
 Fri 08-14  6:30 to promise  █████████████████████████████████████████
#+end_example

One number, and it is the same one the verdict states for today: *free time,
less what is already promised, less the reserve*. Positive is what may still
be taken on; negative is what would have to come off the day first. There is
no second definition of "load" living in a table of its own — a figure that
means something slightly different from the one three lines above it is worse
than no figure at all.

Today appears as the first row, drawn at the same length as the bar in the
capacity block. That is the anchor: a day is only "busy" relative to another
day, and the eye does that comparison for free when the two pictures match.

Capacity is worked out only for the days actually drawn
(=org-foresight-load-rows=, five by default) rather than for the whole
horizon.

* Placement

=M-x org-foresight-plan-fill= proposes times for work that has been accepted
but never given a place to happen — unscheduled tasks, and tasks scheduled for
today with no time on them.

#+begin_example
free 5:45 · surge 1:00 · budget 4:45

  09:00–09:30   0:30  Prepare the board pack  [no estimate]
  09:45–11:45   2:00  Draft next quarter's objectives
  11:50–12:05   0:15  Chase the invoice
  ...
  1:30  Review the migration plan       no budget left
#+end_example

The reserve comes out before anything is placed, so what it proposes is a day
that survives an ordinary number of interruptions rather than one that only
works if nothing happens. Nearest deadline goes first; among equals, the
shortest job, which clears the most open loops per hour.

Two things it will not do: split a task across a meeting, and write anything
without being asked. Proposals open in a review buffer — =d= drops one,
=C-c C-c= writes the rest — and whatever did not fit is listed with the reason
rather than quietly dropped.

* Trying it without real data

#+begin_src
M-x org-foresight-demo-mode
#+end_src

Generates a week's worth of tasks, meetings, clock history and one example of
every signal, points the agenda at it, and puts everything back when switched
off. The data is written relative to today rather than stored with fixed
dates, so it describes the same situation whenever it is generated —
=M-x org-foresight-demo-regenerate= is also the way back to a clean slate
after experimenting.

Real files are never touched: fabricated clock entries would otherwise enter
every report and every learned average, with no undo.
