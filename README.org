#+title: org-foresight
#+options: toc:nil

Forward-looking capacity, signals and scheduling for Org.

Org records the past well and the future badly. Clock reports, logbooks and
agenda logs all answer /what happened/; almost nothing answers /what can still
be promised/. =org-foresight= adds that missing axis.

* What it answers

- *How much can I still take on today?* — available time, minus what is already
  committed, minus a reserve for work that has not arrived yet.
- *When will I be done?* — the remaining commitment poured into the gaps between
  meetings, which gives a finish time rather than a hope.
- *When could I take this on?* — the same calculation over the next fortnight,
  which is what turns "I'm busy" into a date.

* First use

** See it working before configuring anything

#+begin_src
M-x org-foresight-demo-mode
#+end_src

Generates a week of tasks, meetings, travel, clock history and one example of
every signal; points the agenda at it; and puts everything back when switched
off. Real files are never touched. Then =C-c a a= for the day, and
=M-x org-foresight-board= for what has not been settled.

=M-x org-foresight-demo-regenerate= returns the demo to a clean slate after
experimenting.

** Where the settings go

In an =init.el= or a module, not =custom.el=. Every value below says something
about how you work, and the reasoning is worth keeping next to it — Customize
records the value and loses the reason.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(use-package org-foresight
  :after org-agenda
  :config
  ;; The shape of an ordinary day.  A declaration, not an observation: the
  ;; end time is the hour you intend to stop, so that there is something to
  ;; defend.  M-x org-foresight-shape-day declares an unusual one.
  ;;
  ;; Work is a list, so a day may break -- for lunch, for a school run, for
  ;; anything.  The gap is not quiet working time: nothing is planned into it
  ;; and it is not counted as capacity.
  (setq org-foresight-awake    '("06:30" . "23:00")
        org-foresight-work     '(("09:00" . "12:00")
                                 ("13:00" . "17:30"))
        org-foresight-workdays '(1 2 3 4 5))

  ;; Where entries happen, and what moving between them costs.  Without this
  ;; no journey is ever counted and every office meeting looks an hour long.
  (setq org-foresight-places '((office . "HQ\\|Meeting Room")
                               (client . "Client\\|Site"))
        org-foresight-home-place 'home
        org-foresight-travel-matrix '(((home . office) . 60)
                                      ((office . client) . 45))
        org-foresight-travel-default 45)

  ;; The two ends of the day, off by default because they are a habit rather
  ;; than something the calendar knows.  Ten minutes on arrival to see what
  ;; the day is, and ten before leaving to see what only being here can
  ;; settle.  The keys are resolved when the row is drawn.
  (setq org-foresight-check-in
        '(:minutes 10 :title "look at the day \\[org-agenda-list]")
        org-foresight-check-out
        '(:minutes 10 :title "before you leave \\[org-foresight-board]"))

  ;; The agenda's left gutter carries the margin and two brackets -- the
  ;; working hours and the hours away from home -- so the prefix needs five
  ;; leading spaces.  The effort goes in front of `% s': an estimate is a
  ;; number read down a column, and behind the leader it moves with whatever
  ;; word that row needed -- `Scheduled: ', `Deadline:  ', or nothing at all.
  (setq org-agenda-prefix-format
        '((agenda . "     %-8.8c%?-12t%?-5e% s")))

  ;; Categories that are life rather than work.  They fill the day but never
  ;; count against capacity, and are never reported as work that escaped.
  (setq org-foresight-private-categories '("family" "personal"))

  ;; Meetings from this category imply preparation and follow-up.
  (setq org-foresight-meeting-categories '("work"))

  ;; Work that arrives rather than is planned carries `SURGE', and the value
  ;; is when it arrived.  Nothing writes it for you -- add it to whatever
  ;; captures an interruption, or use `M-x org-foresight-mark-surge':
  ;;
  ;; (add-to-list
  ;;  'org-capture-templates
  ;;  `("i" "interrupt" entry (file+datetree my-journal)
  ;;    ,(concat "* ONGO %?\n:PROPERTIES:\n:SURGE: %U\n:END:\n")
  ;;    :clock-in t :clock-resume t)
  ;;  t)

  ;; Where generated tasks and per-day declarations are written.
  (setq org-foresight-task-file (expand-file-name "journal.org" org-directory)
        org-foresight-day-file  (expand-file-name "journal.org" org-directory)))
#+end_src

That is the whole of it: values, and nothing to wire. Loading the package is
the installation — the derived rows, the brackets and the report block all
attach themselves to the agenda, and =org-foresight-report-style= set to nil
is how the last of them is turned off again. When the verdict says something
is outstanding, =M-x org-foresight-board= shows it.

The board can be reached from the dispatcher as well, since it takes the
match string every custom command is handed and ignores it:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(add-to-list 'org-agenda-custom-commands
             '("b" "Board" org-foresight-board ""))
#+end_src

There are two views and one board, and they ask different questions: the day
(=C-c a a=), the week just gone, and what has not been settled. A view that
draws the same agenda with a different block underneath is not a third place
— it is a toggle, and it will not be opened.

=M-x org-foresight-diagnose= then reports what is set, what is having no
effect, and what to do next. Worth running once after the above and again
whenever a figure looks wrong.

What to look for: the office meeting costs its journey either side, the remote

as unplannable because it has no estimate.

** Once there is history

Two figures are learned rather than configured, so they need running once and
then occasionally:

| Command                       | When                | What it corrects              |
|-------------------------------+---------------------+-------------------------------|
| =org-foresight-learn-surge= | weekly              | how much of a day arriving work takes |
| =org-foresight-learn-leak=  | weekly              | how much a day loses to the unrecorded |
| =org-foresight-learn-bias=  | monthly             | how far estimates run over    |

The watcher is read hourly rather than on every redraw: one fetch is four
requests returning a day of events each, paid inside a redraw you are waiting
on, and what they buy is an account of hours already spent. =C-u r= asks it
again now, as does =M-x org-foresight-observe-refresh=.

The rules, the entries, =now=, the categories, the effort column — all of
that is Org. Five things are not:

| Row                | What it is                                        |
|--------------------+---------------------------------------------------|
| =→ office=         | a journey derived from where the day happens      |
| =0:30 free=        | a stretch nothing has claimed, in the colour of room |
| =↳ NEXT …=         | promised work that would fit in what is left of the gap above it, and can be done from where that gap is |
| =work starts ────= | the edge of the working day                       |
| =work lands ────=  | where the day actually ends, corrections included |

The first two edges are declarations — the hours being defended — and the
third is what the day is going to do with them. Same subject, one verb apart,
because the difference between intending to stop at half five and stopping at
half five is the subject of this whole package. It is drawn only when it
disagrees with the declaration, in the overrun's colour past the edge and the
colour of room before it: landing early is not a lesser kind of news. A day
too full to end at all still gets the rule, at the last hour there is and
reading =work lands · 4:15 will not fit= — a quantity of work with nowhere
left to go, counting the evening in, not an hour. The day the projection
matters most is not the day to go silent on.

Four marks, explained by a key under the bars — and only when the day used
them, because a key describing a clash on a day that has none is explaining a
problem the reader does not have:

| Mark | Meaning                                                       |
|------+---------------------------------------------------------------|
| =@=  | needs a place today does not go — so it is offered in no gap  |
| =⨯=  | will not fit, or is booked twice — a journey that cannot have the hours it needs, work with no gap big enough to take it, or an hour two things have both claimed (both of them are marked, since it takes two) |
| =↳=  | would fit in the gap above                                    |
| =↯=  | arrived today — it landed on the day rather than was planned into it |
| =╰=  | shares its time — see [[*Not everything wants all of you]]             |

Each is drawn in the colour of what it says, in the rows as in the key --
except the last two, which are drawn in no colour at all. The others each
carry a decision: the overrun's colour says this has to move, the colour of
room says here is somewhere it could go. Sharing an hour asks for nothing, so
it is grey, and a reader who has learnt two colours is not asked to learn a
third.

The first two are the two ways a day says /not today/, and they are the two
that survive when a row would otherwise take a quieter mark — not fitting is
answered by moving work about, needing a place by going there or by waiting
until you are.

Every mark goes in one column: the one the grid's headings begin at, which is
where the time field ends. That is left of a =Scheduled:= and left of an
effort, so a mark stays beside the clock and the title it qualifies instead
of wandering right by however long a particular row's leader happened to be.
Undated work has no clock in its prefix at all — a conditional time field is
dropped from such a row rather than padded — so it takes its own heading
instead. Only marked rows are widened; what has to line up is the marks, not
the titles, which a leader or an effort moves anyway.

One glyph serves both kinds of not-fitting, because they are the same news
and the row says which is which. U+2A2F rather than a ballot or multiplication cross — PlemolJP has no glyph
for U+2715, U+2717 or U+2718, so each arrives from the fallback font at
whatever width that font uses and the column behind it steps, and U+00D7 is
present but full width, which steps by a whole cell.

None of them is written to a file, and none of them is placed for you.

*** Why Org draws it

The agenda already is the day: the time grid interleaves its rules with every
timed entry, in order, with =now= in its place. Drawing a second column beside
it would mean drawing the day twice — and the copy would lose the log lines,
the habit graphs, the tags, the dimming of blocked tasks, and every command
that already works on a real agenda line.

So the derived rows are handed to Org rather than drawn: each is built by
`org-agenda-format-item' with a time and no entry behind it — exactly how Org
builds its own grid rules — and added before `org-agenda-finalize-entries'
sorts. Org puts each one where its clock says it goes. Nothing here reproduces
Org's ordering, and nothing breaks when a sorting option changes.

*Every added row must carry a time.* That is the whole contract, and it is
worth stating because the failure is quiet: `org-cmp-time' reads the
=time-of-day= property and treats a row without one as later than everything,
and since the sort is stable such rows come out in the order they were added
— in a heap at the foot of the buffer, outside the day they describe. A test
holds every row to it.

A journey that has been squeezed is drawn at the length it *needs* rather than
at what was left for it, marked =⨯=. Fifteen minutes shown for a forty-five
minute drive is the day claiming to work when it does not; drawn honestly it
overlaps the meeting it collides with, which is where the collision is.

A candidate row carries the marker of the entry it names, so =s=, =S-right=,
=>=, =e=, =t= and =RET= work on it. That is the point of showing candidates at
the hour a gap opens: the answer to "where does this go" is given by putting
the cursor on the line and pressing the key you would have pressed anyway.

Set =org-foresight-agenda-inject= to nil to see the agenda exactly as Org
builds it.

*** The gap, net of the reserve

A gap is offered at what survives interruption rather than at what the clock
says: =free 3:15 · 2:53 after the reserve=.

Interruptions do not happen at a fixed hour, so putting the reserve down as a
block somewhere would be a claim about the day that is not true. What *is*
true is that they scale with time worked — so every gap is offered at the
fraction of itself that history says survives, and the reserve stops being an
abstraction subtracted somewhere out of sight.

Nothing is placed either way. The gap is only being reported honestly, and
what goes in it stays entirely yours to decide. =org-foresight-gap-net= nil
offers you the clock instead.

*** What will not fit at all

=⨯= marks work with no gap today big enough to hold it.

That is a fact about the work and the day rather than about any arrangement of
them: if an estimate is longer than the largest gap there is, no ordering will
find it a home. Being decidable without placing anything is what keeps the
choice of where things go with you. The day-level version of the same question
— everything fits individually but not together — is the verdict's =OVER by=,
and the line under it.

*** The left edge

One rule, three levels, for the blocks org-foresight adds above and below the
listing:

| Column | What sits there                                              |
|--------+--------------------------------------------------------------|
|      0 | badges, and nothing else                                     |
|      1 | everything a section contains: verdicts, keys, bars, rows     |
|     2+ | subordinate to the line above — a signal's findings under its heading |

A badge is read by *scanning* rather than by reading, so it gets the frame
edge to itself: an eye running down the left of the buffer hits section
headings and nothing else. Everything a section contains starts one column
in. Depth past that means one thing only: this line belongs to the one above
it.

These are the blocks' own columns and are not tied to the agenda's prefix,
which is the reader's to set and carries the two brackets besides. A page
whose listing begins further in is not a page whose headings should move: the
badges are a column down the frame, and a frame that shifted with a setting
would stop being one.

That is why the bars are not flush left. A bar is not a label; it is the
picture of the numbers beside it, and it belongs at the same margin as the
words it illustrates.

** What has to come out

The mirror of the same idea sits under the verdict. A bar says how far over
the day is; it can never say by what. So an overcommitted day names its way
out:

: Work 8:30 · OVER by 0:45 · ends 18:15
: ↳ any one of Quarterly summary 1:00 · Project review 1:30

Ranked by the *smallest that is on its own enough* — the opposite order to
the gap's, and for the same reason: there you want the most in, here you want
to give up the least. Where nothing alone is enough, the largest are taken in
turn until they are (=↳ needs all of=), because the question has stopped
being which one and become how many. Where even everything movable falls
short, it says so outright:

: ↳ only 3:00 of it can move: Draft the summary 1:30 · Standup 0:30

which is a different problem with a different answer. A day whose overflow is
made of other people's meetings cannot be rescheduled out of trouble, and
knowing that at 09:00 is the difference between declining something and
working late.

Meetings and dated tasks are candidates alongside promised work — a day is
rarely saved by moving only the things nobody else knows about. Travel is
not, because it is not moved: it follows whatever it was getting you to.
Neither is anything private or =informational=. An overfilled *working* day
is not answered by moving the family, and a fixture nobody asked you to
attend was never yours to move. =org-foresight-grid-frees= sets how many are
named, or =nil= for none.

Deadlines are Org's own — =org-agenda-deadline-leaders= writes =In 3 d.:= in
front of the entry, which asks the same question in the same column as
everything else.

** A note on the block characters

Three shapes, and each says something the colour cannot.

| Shape        | What it is                 | Segments                          |
|--------------+----------------------------+-----------------------------------|
| filled block | claimed                    | booked, travel, promised, private |
| outline      | held open, holding nothing | reserve                           |
| dots         | not claimed at all         | spare, unclaimed, free            |

Only the claimed hours need telling apart from each other, so only they spend
the grey ramp — three steps, two apart, which is as fine as an eye reliably
resolves. Everything else is a shape, and shapes are read faster than shades.
(The examples render the outline as =▭= for want of a way to draw a face box
in a text file; on screen it is one rectangle around the whole run, in the
same yellow as =┃= and =OVER by=. The reserve is the last thing between a day
and an overrun, so spending it *is* being over without having said so.)

Nothing is lost by drawing the unclaimed part in dots, because a horizontal
bar carries its quantity in *length*, not in ink — the run is the same length
either way. What is gained is that the one thing on the board worth growing
stops shouting: a saturated hue at full block density outweighs the same area
of grey by far more than its share of the day, so a bar with plenty of room
in it reads as the busiest of the lot.

Shading the segments with =▓ ▒ ░= would be the obvious choice and is the
wrong one: in several monospace fonts those sit on a different box from =█=
— in PlemolJP, =▓= is 74 units high and 55 left of it on a 1000 em, and
narrower besides — so every seam between them visibly steps.

The part-width blocks =▏▎▍▌▋▊▉= are kept for what they are actually for: the
last cell of a run, where the duration does not fill a whole one. They share
the full block's box exactly, and nothing follows them to be pushed out of
line.

One thing to check in your own font: =█= is normally drawn to *overflow* its
em box, because the glyph exists to tile a screen without seams. Where it
does, a bar stands visibly taller than boxed text on the same line, and two
bars set one under the other fuse into one mass. Measure it — in an unpatched
PlemolJP the block runs 1300 units against a 1175-unit line, i.e. ~10% proud
at the bottom. Fonts that normalise tile glyphs to the line box (as a patched
PlemolJP NF does, at 890/−210) draw the bar flush with everything else.
That is a property of the font, not of this package.

* Looking back

The foot of =C-c a a= answers the half no plan can supply: what the day cost
against what it was said to cost.

Every figure is named for what it is. *Clocked* is Org's own clock total —
not time at the machine, which is the number it is measured against. A
*spell* is one unbroken run of it, and the longest is the honest answer to
whether the day held together. *vs 7d* compares with the last week's daily
average, so it says whether today was ordinary; it is not the reserve.

Two rows share one axis. The first is where the activity was, coloured by
whether a clock was running. The second is *when the day got chopped up* — a
count of switches says a day was fragmented, but only the row says at which
hours, and that is the part anybody can act on.

Each task row gives what it cost against what it was estimated at, as a
single percentage. 100% is exactly on the estimate. One notation throughout:
a multiplier for the ones that overran and a percentage for the rest would
make two columns out of one, and leave the eye to work out which it was
reading. The rows carry their entries' markers, so =RET= visits the task.

*leak* and *lost* divide the time no clock accounts for, by whether you were
at the keyboard:

: ↳ leak 0:52 · Slack 0:20 · Mail 0:18 · Terminal 0:14
: ↳ lost 0:40 · away from the machine

=leak= is active and unrecorded: work that happened and went unwritten, or the
small unnamed handling a day fills up with. =lost= is idle — away, or reading,
or in a conversation, which input alone cannot tell apart.

Not divided by application. What was on screen says nothing about whether the
hour was work, and dividing by it only moves the guess somewhere harder to see.
The applications are still named under =leak=, because what you were typing
into while not recording it is worth seeing; under =lost= they are not, because
you were not there. The parts add up to the total, and what will not fit is
counted with =+N= rather than dropped.

Without an =aw-watcher-afk= there is no record of when the machine was
attended, only of when a window was in front. The day is then drawn from
that instead, and the header says =screen only=: a coarse picture is worth
more than a blank one, but the two must not look alike.

* Not everything wants all of you

Occupying an hour and demanding all of it are different things, and treating
them as one makes a full day look impossible.

| =:ATTENTION:=   | Costs the hour | Shares it | Example                    |
|-----------------+----------------+-----------+----------------------------|
| (unset)         | yes            | no        | a meeting you run          |
| =background=    | yes            | yes       | a call you only listen to  |
| =informational= | no             | yes       | a child's fixture          |

Both are marked =╰= in the agenda, so an overlap between one of them and real
work reads as what it is rather than as a clash to be resolved.
=M-x org-foresight-set-attention= sets it from the agenda line — the judgement
is made while looking at the day, so it is made from there.

A =background= entry keeps its hour but hangs beneath whatever else is
happening then, so listening on the commute stops being reported as a day
that cannot happen. An =informational= one takes nothing at all: it stays in
the agenda where it belongs, but claims none of the day and displaces no work
— a child's practice is not work, though it is exactly what decides when the
house is empty.

Set it per entry, or by category for things that are always so:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq org-foresight-informational-categories '("club"))
#+end_src

Then work down the board. Every row answers to the agenda's own commands, and
each edit moves the figures above it straight away:

| Key                     | What it does                     |
|-------------------------+----------------------------------|
| =s=                     | schedule it on another day       |
| =S-left= / =S-right=    | a day earlier or later           |
| =C-u S-left/right=      | an hour earlier or later         |
| =C-u C-u S-left/right=  | by =org-time-stamp-rounding-minutes= |
| =>=                     | prompt for a date, or a time     |
| =e=                     | estimate it                      |
| =t=                     | change its state                 |
| =RET=                   | visit the entry                  |

The time keys are what clear a =⨯=. They work because a booked row puts
=org-marker= on its timestamp rather than its heading, which is the agenda's
own convention — the commands go there and insist on finding a stamp. Set
=org-time-stamp-rounding-minutes= to =(0 15)= if five-minute steps are finer
than you think in.

- *Over?* Move something off today — the verdict names what would be enough,
  and the load table says which day can take it. Or move it *within* today,
  which is usually cheaper.
- *Spare?* Pull something in from the signals.

*During the day* — the first line of =C-c a a=. If =spare= is small, the
answer to a new request is a date, not a yes.

*Before leaving* — whatever review command you already use.

* The day

A day is not one bar from nine to half five. Work and private life are mixed in
time, some of it is spent getting somewhere, and the hours that belong to
neither are not absent — they are time that happens to be unclaimed.

Work itself need not be one stretch. Declaring =(("09:00" . "12:00") ("13:00"
. "17:30"))= takes the lunch hour out of the span, out of the gaps that may be
offered, and out of the projection: the day is drawn with =work pauses= and
=work resumes= around it, and nothing plans through it.

#+begin_example
 Work 8:30 · 1:15 left to promise · ends 16:45 · reserve 0:24 of 1:00 · est +0:40
            █ booked 2:45  █ travel 1:00  █ promised 2:30  ▭ reserve 1:00
            · spare 1:15
 Work  8:30 ██████████████████████████████▭▭▭▭▭······
 Off   7:00 ███████··························
            █ private 1:30  · unclaimed 5:30
#+end_example

Two bars, written as the sums they are, so the parts can be checked against
the whole without adding them up and hoping. They share one scale: an hour is
the same length in both, which is what lets a working day be set against the
hours it leaves behind.

| Segment    | What it is                                          |
|------------+-----------------------------------------------------|
| =booked=   | meetings and work already at a time                 |
| =travel=   | getting to and from them                            |
| =private=  | life that falls inside working hours                |
| =promised= | effort accepted without a time yet                  |
| =reserve=  | held for arriving work, unrecorded time, and time away |
| =spare=    | the span less all five — what may still be promised |

The =Off= bar accounts for the rest of the waking day — before work, between
its intervals, and after it: =private= for commitments outside working hours,
=unclaimed= for the hours nothing has claimed. Work that lands out there is
not absorbed silently — it appears as =borrowed=, and says so.

*** The spine

The working hours are bracketed down the agenda's left edge, one column in —
the margin every block's body starts at, because the listing is a body like
any other and the frame edge belongs to the badges:

#+begin_example
 ┌          9:00...... work starts ────────────
 │          9:00-9:30  0:30 free · 0:29 usable
 │ meeting  9:30-9:45  Standup
 └         12:00...... work pauses ────────────
           12:15-12:45 Vendor call            ← outside the bracket
 ┌         13:00...... work resumes ────────────
 │         13:00-17:30 4:30 free · 4:25 usable
 └         17:30...... work ends ────────────
           19:00-20:30 Dinner
#+end_example

A day in pieces reads as pieces: two stretches of work are two brackets, and
what falls in the declared break sits visibly outside them. The corners are
what make that work — a plain bar would say only that some rows are work,
without saying where a stretch begins.

The glyph *replaces* the blank in that column rather than being inserted, so
no column moves; where that column is not blank, the line is left alone.
=org-foresight-agenda-spine= turns it off.

*** The second bracket — where the body is

Beside it, two columns to the right, the hours spent away from home. Home is
unmarked: it is the state the day returns to, and marking it would mark most
of every day.

#+begin_example
 ┌            9:00...... work starts ────────────
 │   travel   9:00-10:00 → office       ← the road belongs to neither end
 │ ┌ check   10:00-10:10 look at the day
 │ │ meeting 11:00-11:30 Standup
 │ │        12:00...... work pauses ────────────
 │ └ check   16:20-16:30 before you leave
 │   travel  16:30-17:30 → home
 └           17:30...... work ends ────────────
#+end_example

Both journeys fall outside it, and that is the point: an hour spent getting
somewhere is not an hour spent there, and a bracket drawn around the drive
would offer those minutes for work that needs the place. An errand covering a
single row is drawn =├=, a bracket with both corners in one cell.

It needs a column of its own and a blank after it, so the prefix wants five
leading spaces — the margin, two brackets, the blank between them, and the
usual gap before the text:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq org-agenda-prefix-format '((agenda . "     %-8.8c%?-12t% s%?-5e")))
#+end_src

Without them nothing is drawn — all or nothing, decided once from the
formatter rather than line by line, because a bracket that appeared on a day
of hour lines and vanished on a day with entries in it would read as a fault
rather than as a setting nobody turned on.
=org-foresight-agenda-place-spine= turns it off outright.

The first bracket asks less: any prefix leaves its column blank, and three
leading spaces are enough to keep it clear of the text.

An overcommitted day is drawn past the width with =┃= where the span actually
ended, rather than being scaled back until it fits. The one day that must not
look workable is the one that is not.

"I stop at 17:30" is therefore not the end of a window. It is that the hours
after it are already spoken for.

*** Where the day is worked from

A day has a place as well as hours, and it is the one thing no entry can say:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq org-foresight-day-places '((1 . office) (3 . office))  ; Mon and Wed
      org-foresight-home-place 'home)                        ; the rest
#+end_src

A day that goes differently says so on its own heading, next to =WORK=:

#+begin_example
*** 2026-08-17 Mon
:PROPERTIES:
:WORK:  09:00-12:00 13:00-17:30
:PLACE: office
:END:
#+end_example

A place is a closed set of names, and a misspelt one is not an error
anywhere — it simply stops matching, and the day goes on quietly being
worked from home. So it is worth letting Org offer the names:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setf (alist-get "PLACE_ALL" org-global-properties nil nil #'equal)
      "home office client")
#+end_src

=C-c C-x p= now completes =PLACE= wherever it is typed — on a day's heading
and on the occasional entry whose =LOCATION= does not say where the body has
to be. Global rather than a =#+PROPERTY:= line, so it holds in an imported
calendar too, which a sync is free to rewrite.

Two things follow.

The commute becomes a cost of the day that has it, *inside the working
hours*. Where the work happens is not where the body starts — an office day
is still begun and ended at home — so the day carries a journey each way:

#+begin_example
office day, nothing in the calendar:
  travel  09:00-10:00  → office
  travel  16:30-17:30  → home
  span 8:30 · travel 2:00 · spare 6:30      (a home day: spare 8:30)
#+end_example

Two hours, whether or not anything is in the calendar. That is the number
worth having before agreeing to go in for its own sake — and it only appears
if the journeys are counted as work. Put them outside the hours and going in
leaves the same working day as staying home, while quietly eating the
morning: the one arrangement that hides the cost entirely.

And work that needs a place stops being merely late: it is not late until the
next day at that place has gone, which is what =Here= on the board is able to
say.

*** People, and what actually binds you

=:PEOPLE:= names who a piece of work involves, as a multi-valued property
(=:PEOPLE: 佐藤 田中=, and =:PEOPLE+:= to add). The relation comes from the
entry's own state: =WAIT= and =DELEG= are /with/ them, anything else /needs/
them.

It deliberately does not bind a place. Most work involving somebody can be
done by message, and what makes a conversation need a room is a judgement
about that conversation — that it would go wrong in writing — which nothing
here can infer. When it does need a room, say so the way anything else does:

#+begin_example
* NEXT ask about the spec          * NEXT talk about the review
:PROPERTIES:                       :PROPERTIES:
:PEOPLE: 佐藤                      :PEOPLE: 佐藤
:END:                              :PLACE:  office
                                   :END:
#+end_example

The first is a message and goes anywhere. Only the second appears under
=Here=.

The hours are *declared*, never inferred. Measuring when work actually stopped
and calling that the working day would teach the system to plan around the
overruns it exists to prevent. =M-x org-foresight-shape-day= declares an
unusual day on its own heading; ordinary days need no input at all.

Run from an agenda line it shapes *the day under the cursor*, and redraws.
That is the case worth binding a key to: the day that goes differently is
almost never today, and a command that could only shape today would have to
be remembered on the morning it was too late to plan around.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(define-key org-agenda-mode-map "D" #'org-foresight-shape-day)
#+end_src

A day's own heading may carry =WAKE=, =SLEEP= and =WORK=:

#+begin_example
*** 2026-08-10 Mon
:PROPERTIES:
:WAKE:  06:00
:SLEEP: 22:00
:WORK:  09:00-12:00 13:00-17:30
:END:
#+end_example

=WORK= takes one range, several separated by spaces or commas, or =none= for a
day with no work in it at all.

* Travel is work

Whether an employer counts the commute as paid time is beside the point: an
hour spent getting to a meeting is an hour of your life spent on that meeting.
Journeys are derived from where entries actually are and booked like anything
else.

#+begin_example
the same one-hour meeting, held remotely:   free 7:30 · spare 6:30
                          held at the office: free 5:30 · spare 4:30
#+end_example

A journey is work, so it is placed inside the working hours. On a day that
breaks, the search steps past the break exactly as it steps past a meeting: an
hour's drive to a one o'clock meeting is taken out of the working hour before
lunch, not out of lunch. What cannot fit — the drive that has to begin before
work starts — is still drawn, and counted as =borrowed=.

*** Where a journey sits

One rule: *a journey is placed as close as it can be to the moment that pins
it.* Usually what is pinned is the arrival — something needs you there — so
the journey is the last slot that still gets you there in time, sliding
earlier past whatever is already booked.

A day worked from somewhere else is the case that shows the rule has two
ends. Nothing needs you at nine o'clock in particular; what needs you is the
day. So the *departure* is what is pinned, and the journey is the first thing
the working day does:

| The day (work 09:00–17:30, an hour away) | Out           | Back                |
|------------------------------------------+---------------+---------------------|
| office day, nothing booked               | 09:00–10:00   | 16:30–17:30         |
| office day, meeting there 15:00          | 09:00–10:00   | 16:30–17:30         |
| office day, meeting there 09:30          | 08:30–09:30 /borrowed/ | 16:30–17:30 |
| office day, meeting there 17:00–18:00    | 09:00–10:00   | 18:00–19:00 /borrowed/ |
| home day, meeting there 11:00–12:00      | 10:00–11:00   | 12:00–13:00         |
| the same, with lunch declared 12:00–13:00 | 10:00–11:00  | 13:00–14:00         |

Rows three and four are the rule choosing its other end: something needs you
there before you could arrive, or holds you past the point where being home
in time is possible. In both the journey spills out of the declared day and
is shown as =borrowed=, because that is what it is.

The last two rows are the other half of it. On a day worked from home, going
to the office is an excursion: *nothing keeps you there once the thing that
took you there is over*, so you leave at noon rather than sitting at a desk
you have no reason to be at until half four. And the way back is no more
allowed into the declared lunch hour than the way out was — it waits for the
afternoon, and the cost lands on working time, where it belongs.

On a day worked *from* the office the same errand behaves the other way
round: you go back to the office, because the rest of the day still happens
there — unless getting back would land after the moment you would have to set
off home anyway, in which case going back is a journey to nowhere and you go
straight home.

Only a place someone wrote down counts. Every imported invitation carries a
video link, and a link says nothing about where the body has to be — so an
unhelpful =LOCATION= leaves you where you already were rather than inventing a
journey. Set =org-foresight-places= to map locations onto places, and
=org-foresight-travel-matrix= to say what moving between them costs.

*** A free hour in the wrong place

The other half of knowing where the body is: a gap only offers the work that
can be done in it. A task carrying =:PLACE: office= is suggested in the hours
actually spent at the office — after the journey in and before the journey
back — and in no others. An hour free at home is no use to it at all, and a
suggestion that ignored that would be worse than none: it reads as an answer,
gets acted on, and the hour goes on finding out that it was not one.

Note that this is where the body is minute by minute, not where the day is
worked from. On an office day the morning before you set off is still spent at
home, and the evening after you get back is too.

What is left, not what there was. A stretch that has gone offers nothing,
whatever it held this morning, and one that is half gone offers what still
fits in front of it: an hour suggested at two o'clock for a gap that opened at
nine is not an offer. The row above still reports the stretch as it was —
the shape of the day is what it was — and only the offer is about what is
still ahead. What became of the morning is a different question, and =v l=
answers it with what was finished in it.

*** When the derived journey is wrong

It is a claim about the day, and a good one until the day disagrees. The train
you actually catch, an errand on the way, going in early because the road is
quieter: none of that can be guessed from where a meeting happens to be.

=M-x org-foresight-book-travel= on a =→ office= row asks when you are leaving
and writes the journey down:

#+begin_example
,* → office
<2026-08-19 Wed 07:10--2026-08-19 Wed 08:10>
:PROPERTIES:
:TRAVEL: office
:END:
#+end_example

From then on it is an entry like any other -- Org draws it, it is counted as
travel, and it puts you at the office from the moment it ends -- and nothing
derives a second leg to a place you have already said you are going to. The
way back is still derived, since nobody wrote that down.

It has a command of its own rather than answering to =s=. A derived row
answers to none of Org's commands, because there is no entry behind it and
inheriting a neighbour's marker would quietly reschedule the wrong thing. One
key meaning /make this real/ is honest; one key meaning two different things
depending on the row is not.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(define-key org-agenda-mode-map "T" #'org-foresight-book-travel)
#+end_src

*** Somewhere you cannot work

A journey normally arrives just in time. Leaving at the last possible moment
is what a person does, and the hours before it belong to wherever they already
were — which is right for anywhere work can happen, and wrong for the gym:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq org-foresight-unworkable-places '(gym))
#+end_src

#+begin_example
without                              with
  travel   9:10-9:30  → gym           travel   9:10-9:30  → gym
  persona  9:30-10:30 swim            persona  9:30-10:30 swim
          10:30-14:20 3:50 free       travel  10:30-11:10 → office
  ↳ NEXT ordinary work                        11:10-15:00 3:50 free
  travel  14:20-15:00 → office        ↳ NEXT ordinary work
#+end_example

The same journey and the same free minutes, moved so that they are somewhere
they can be spent. Nothing is offered in an unworkable place either way.

Deliberately not a ranking. The only question a ranking would settle beyond
this one is where to go when nothing needs you anywhere, and that already has
an answer: back to where the day is worked from.

Being left out is silent, though, and silence is indistinguishable from a bug.
So work needing a place today never reaches carries =@= in the mark column,
and the key under the bars says what it means. On a day that does go there the
mark is absent — the work can be done, and the board's =Here= section lists
it:

#+begin_example
 home, office · next home day is Wed 08-19
    pick up the signed contract           @office
#+end_example

=Here= asks the same question, so the two can never disagree: a day based at
home with one appointment at the office is a day on which the office errands
can, in fact, be run — and the day to be told so, since by tomorrow the answer
is Wednesday again.

What it will not do is *make* the hour by moving the commute. Journeys are
derived from commitments that name a place, and a journey invented to fit a
task in would mean adding a =NEXT= changed what time you left the house. Where
the work needs a place the day does not have, the answer is the board's =Here=
section — it lists exactly that work, says when that place comes round again,
and marks =⚠= where a deadline falls before then. Going in is a decision, and
=M-x org-foresight-shape-day= on the day in question is where it gets
recorded.

*** The two ends of the day

Looking at the day when you arrive, and looking at it again before you go, are
not overhead — they are the two moments the rest of this package is for. They
take ten minutes each and appear nowhere, which means those twenty minutes
come out of something else every single day, silently. The same arithmetic as
an unbooked commute.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq org-foresight-check-in
      '(:minutes 10 :title "look at the day \\[org-agenda-list]")
      org-foresight-check-out
      '(:minutes 10 :title "before you leave \\[org-foresight-board]"))
#+end_src

Both are nil by default. Unlike a journey this is not derived from anything
written down: whether a day opens with a ritual, and how long it takes, is a
fact about a person rather than about their calendar.

The title is passed through =substitute-command-keys=, so it can name the way
to do the thing and go on naming the right key after the key has moved.

*Where they go is the whole of the design.* The journeys are settled first,
and a check takes the first or last ten minutes the day has left:

| The day (work 09:00–17:30, an hour away) | In            | Out                 |
|------------------------------------------+---------------+---------------------|
| home day                                 | 09:00–09:10   | 17:20–17:30         |
| office day                               | 10:00–10:10   | 16:20–16:30         |
| office day, meeting there 17:00–18:00    | 10:00–10:10   | 16:50–17:00         |
| office day, solid from 13:00             | 10:00–10:10   | 11:50–12:00         |

There is no rule about arrivals and departures in that: the journeys are
simply among the things a check may not be drawn over, so on an office day the
first free minutes are the ones after you get there and the last are the ones
before you set off back. The other order was tried and is wrong — checks
first, travel fitted around them, and the check before leaving sits in the
last minutes of the day and pushes the journey home past the end of it. Every
office day would borrow an hour of the evening for a commute that used to fit.

The last row is the same rule on a day with nothing spare in its afternoon,
and the answer is worth having: *ten to twelve is your last chance*, because
after noon you are in meetings and then on the way home. The search runs back
only as far as the working day began — and lands in the same place whether or
not the day happens to declare a lunch break, which was not true when it
stopped at the last stretch of work.

A declared break is no more available to a check than to a drive. A day with
no ten free minutes anywhere gets its check anyway, sitting on whatever is in
the way and marked =⨯=: that day is the one the check would have caught
something on, and it used to disappear from it silently — a band lying
entirely under another is dropped when the day is made into a partition, so
the row went missing from exactly the days that needed it.

* The reserve, and the estimates

Two corrections are learned from history, because neither can be got by asking
harder.

*Interruptions* are not controllable, but their volume is stable and
measurable — and they are measurable in Org alone. An entry carrying the
=SURGE= property arrived rather than was planned, and the property's value is
when it arrived. =M-x org-foresight-learn-surge= sums the minutes clocked
against such work on the day it landed, takes the median over days, and holds
that much back.

Nothing writes the property for you: it comes from whatever captures an
interruption on your machine, or from =M-x org-foresight-mark-surge= after the
fact. It is read with inheritance, so a task broken out of an interruption is
part of it without being marked again.

Dating the mark is what makes it stop. Work that arrived on Monday and is
still being done on Thursday is only unplanned on Monday: a date of its own on
any later day means it has been taken in hand, and from then it is ordinary
promised work. Without that, an interruption absorbed over three days would
hold a reserve against work already on the calendar, and hold it three times
over.

*What a day loses* is the other half, and that one does need
[[https://activitywatch.net/][ActivityWatch]]. =M-x org-foresight-learn-leak= measures the two ways a working
day turns out shorter than the clock says — time at the keyboard that no clock
accounted for, and time away from the machine — and holds those back too.
Without the watcher both are zero, and the day is planned at the length it
claims to have. A machine that has never had one costs nothing: the request
fails at once, the failure is cached, and the block says so in words rather
than showing an empty table. A port that silently drops packets — a VPN, a
corporate firewall — is the slow case, and =org-foresight-observe-timeout=
(2 seconds) is what bounds it; a real answer takes about half of one.

The verdict says the reserve as a remainder against the day's whole allowance
— =reserve 0:24 of 1:00=. It shrinks twice over: by the hours going past, and
by the interruptions that have already landed. So it is smallest exactly when
it matters most, and a figure that vanished once it got small would vanish
precisely then. =0:00 of 1:00= is not an empty line: it says the allowance was
real and is now spent, and the next interruption has nowhere to go.

*Estimates* are wrong in a consistent direction, and the evidence is already in
the file: every finished task that carried an =EFFORT= and was clocked is one
estimate measured against its outcome. =M-x org-foresight-learn-bias= reads
them back and applies what it finds to what is promised and to how long a task
is given when placed. Work that was abandoned rather than carried through is
left out — its clock says nothing about its estimate.

How wrong depends on how big the estimate was, which one multiplier cannot
say. Three minutes over a two-minute guess is ×2.5; the same three minutes over
a two-hour one is ×1.025. So a line is fitted in log space —
=ln(actual) = a + b·ln(estimate)= — which makes the correction a function of
the size:

: factor(est) = exp(a) · (est / pivot)^(b−1)

A slope of 1 is a constant multiplier, and is what this reduces to when there
is too little history to fit anything; =b < 1= is the common case, where small
estimates are the broken ones. The slope is shared across categories and only
the intercept is per-category: how far ahead of yourself you run is a habit of
estimating, while which work runs long is a fact about the work, and the second
needs far less evidence to place. The curve is held to the sizes actually seen
— past the ends of the evidence it goes flat rather than extrapolating — and
the final multiplier is clamped either way.

What the correction cost today is always shown (=est +1:20=), in minutes rather
than as a multiple, so a shrinking day reads as "my estimates are optimistic"
rather than as the tool being pessimistic. Set the lower end of
=org-foresight-bias-factor-range= to 1.0 for the cautious reading, where a
correction may only ever grow an estimate.

A row showing a figure that is not the one in the file says both
(=2:00→2:48=). The estimate stays on the left because that is the number that
was written and the one being questioned; the arrow is the learning. This
applies to Org's own effort column too — where the day is planned on a
corrected figure, the row that printed the estimate says what it is being
treated as, in the face the derived rows use.

=r= sets the fitted line against what it was fitted from, which is the one
place to judge whether to believe it:

#+begin_example
[Estimates] how far each size of estimate runs over
 Estimates run ×2.6 at 0:05 and ×1.4 at 1:00 · slope 0.75 · 128 task(s), 90 days
   0:05  n=9    ×2.6   ██████████████████
   0:15  n=19   ×1.9   █████████████
   0:30  n=28   ×1.4   ██████████
   1:00  n=14   ×1.2   ████████
   2:00  n=6    ×1.0   ███████
#+end_example

That small estimates run over and large ones barely do is not a fact about the
tool. It is a fact about how small things get estimated, and it is actionable
on the very next one.

* The board

Two questions the day cannot answer from inside itself, in one buffer
(=M-x org-foresight-board=). Every row carries its entry's marker, so the
agenda's own commands work on them.

** Here — what only this place can do

#+begin_example
[Here] what only this place can do

 office · next office day is Wed 08-19
  ⚠ stamp the form                     due Tue 08-18
    check the warehouse stock
    talk about the review              (佐藤)
#+end_example

Most work goes anywhere. What lands here is the little that does not — a
thing to pick up, a conversation that would go wrong in writing — and it is
the question worth asking at the door, because being here is the part that
runs out.

The heading is what makes it a decision rather than a list: /next office day
is Wednesday/ says what the alternative to doing it now actually costs. =⚠=
marks the ones whose deadline falls before that day.

Sorted by deadline rather than filtered by it: a file that does not use
deadlines would show nothing at all under a filter, and the question is worth
answering either way.

** Signals

Work that exists but has not been planned for.

| Signal              | What it catches                                          |
|---------------------+----------------------------------------------------------|
| Impossible          | a journey that overlaps a meeting — the day as written    |
|                     | cannot happen, whatever effort is put into it             |
| Outside work hours  | work parked outside the working hours -- in the lunch     |
|                     | break as much as at seven in the evening -- so it is      |
|                     | subtracted from nothing and warns about nothing           |
| Unreadable estimate | an =EFFORT= Org itself cannot parse, which breaks the      |
|                     | agenda outright (see below)                              |
| Cannot be done here | planned for today, but today is worked from somewhere    |
|                     | that cannot do it                                        |
| Won't fit today     | promised for today, and longer than any gap that remains  |
| Meetings, unprepared| an invitation implies preparation and follow-up that no    |
|                     | one has budgeted for                                      |
| Unplannable         | a near deadline with no estimate cannot be placed at all  |
| Gone quiet          | handed-off work whose check-in date has passed            |
| Kept moving         | repeated reschedules — a decision that is not being made  |
| Too much in flight  | several things started at once, each costing the switch    |
|                     | back into the others                                      |
| Borrowed            | too much of the week's work taken out of private time     |
| Leaking             | the reserve itself has grown large enough to be a problem |
| Orphaned prep       | preparation for a meeting that has been cancelled         |
| Undecided           | captured and never decided about (off by default: against |
|                     | a real journal it matched 17% of headings, mostly diary)  |

*The unreadable estimate is a special case*, and the only signal about the
tool rather than about the plan. Org calls =org-duration-to-minutes= on every
=EFFORT= it displays, and that function signals rather than shrugging — so one
heading with =:EFFORT: whenever= takes down the whole of =C-c a a= with a
message naming neither the file nor the heading. The board is built by walking
the files rather than by building an agenda, so it still works when the agenda
does not: when =C-c a a= dies with /Invalid duration format/, =M-x
org-foresight-board= is what tells you which heading to go and fix.
(=2h=, =90= and =0:30= are all fine; it is words and stray punctuation that are
not.)

=M-x org-foresight-prepare-meetings= turns the second of these into real
tasks, filed either side of the meeting. It marks each meeting it handles, so
re-running creates nothing, and each generated task points back at its
meeting, which is what makes the orphan signal possible later.

The board says so itself, on the group heading:

#+begin_example
  Meetings without prep (2) · M-x org-foresight-prepare-meetings

* When, then

The forward view answers the second half of "I'm busy": /then when?/ It draws
the coming working days exactly as today is drawn above — same segments, same
colours, same scale — so the two can be read against each other:

#+begin_example
Load  when I could take this on
 Wed 08-12    OVER by 9:49  ████████████████████████████████████████┃███████…
 Thu 08-13  3:30 to promise  ███████████████████████████████████████
 Fri 08-14  6:30 to promise  █████████████████████████████████████████
#+end_example

One number, and it is the same one the verdict states for today: *free time,
less what is already promised, less the reserve*. Positive is what may still
be taken on; negative is what would have to come off the day first. There is
no second definition of "load" living in a table of its own — a figure that
means something slightly different from the one three lines above it is worse
than no figure at all.

Today appears as the first row, drawn at the same length as the bar in the
capacity block. That is the anchor: a day is only "busy" relative to another
day, and the eye does that comparison for free when the two pictures match.

Capacity is worked out only for the days actually drawn
(=org-foresight-load-rows=, five by default) rather than for the whole
horizon.

* Placement

=M-x org-foresight-plan-fill= proposes times for work that has been accepted
but never given a place to happen — unscheduled tasks, and tasks scheduled for
today with no time on them.

#+begin_example
free 5:45 · reserve 1:00 · budget 4:45

  09:00–09:30   0:30  Prepare the board pack  [no estimate]
  09:45–11:45   2:00  Draft next quarter's objectives
  11:50–12:05   0:15  Chase the invoice
  ...
  1:30  Review the migration plan       no budget left
#+end_example

The reserve comes out before anything is placed, so what it proposes is a day
that survives an ordinary number of interruptions rather than one that only
works if nothing happens. Nearest deadline goes first; among equals, the
shortest job, which clears the most open loops per hour.

Two things it will not do: split a task across a meeting, and write anything
without being asked. Proposals open in a review buffer — =d= drops one,
=C-c C-c= writes the rest — and whatever did not fit is listed with the reason
rather than quietly dropped.

* Trying it without real data

#+begin_src
M-x org-foresight-demo-mode
#+end_src

Generates a week's worth of tasks, meetings, clock history and one example of
every signal, points the agenda at it, and puts everything back when switched
off. The data is written relative to today rather than stored with fixed
dates, so it describes the same situation whenever it is generated —
=M-x org-foresight-demo-regenerate= is also the way back to a clean slate
after experimenting.

Real files are never touched: fabricated clock entries would otherwise enter
every report and every learned average, with no undo.
